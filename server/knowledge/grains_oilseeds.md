# Grains & Deep-Root Oilseeds: Wheat, Corn & Sunflower

## Winter / Spring Wheat
- **Almanac Role**: Best cool-season soil protector, weed suppressor, and fungal/nematode break after summer nightshades or melons.
- **Moisture & Drought Tolerance**: Moderate water demand (**~350–450 mm/season**). Deep fibrous roots capture winter rain and residual nitrate left behind by fertilized tomatoes or corn, cutting spring runoff and wind erosion.
- **Critical Stage**: Most sensitive to drought and heat (>31°C) during **booting, flowering (anthesis), and early grain fill**. Moderate dry-down at ripening actually boosts protein and prevents lodging.
- **Residue Bonus**: Leaves high-carbon straw residue on the field—perfect free mulch for the following summer vegetable row if lightly incorporated or crimped.

## Corn (Maize)
- **Nutrient Hunger**: Heavy nitrogen feeder (**140–200 kg N/ha** equivalent for full grain); always place corn **after** nitrogen-fixing chickpeas or crimson clover rather than after another cereal.
- **Critical Water Window**: Tolerates mild vegetative stress, but drought or >35°C heat during **tasseling and silking (pollination)** permanently cuts kernel set. Keep root moisture **>= 45%** through silking.
- **Windbreak & Intercrop Use**: Tall rows buffer hot desert winds across adjacent tomato/melon beds, reducing foliar evapotranspiration by 10–15%.

## Sunflower
- **Subsoil Scavenger**: Taproot reaches **1.5–2.2 m** deep, breaking compacted plow-pan layers and pulling leached potassium/calcium/nitrate back toward the topsoil.
- **Climate Fit**: High heat and drought resilience once established; thrives in semi-arid and arid summers where shallow-rooted crops wilt.
- **Caution**: Susceptible to *Sclerotinia* white mold in humid dense stands—space rows cleanly and avoid planting sunflower immediately before or after lettuce/beans if white mold has appeared locally.
